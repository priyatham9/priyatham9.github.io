RMP accident insights

What the EPA Risk Management Program accident histories show for chemical plants (NAICS 325): how the reported accident rate moved by year, how severe the reported accidents were, and how the rate differs by industry group. Every number on this page is copied from a CSV in the public ehs-chemical-peers repository, and a test fails if the page drifts from it.

Read the limits box before quoting any number. These are reported accident records, not a measure of how safe a plant is.

!

What these numbers cannot tell you

Read these rates as counts of reported accident records, not as a measure of how safe a plant or an industry is. The sentences below are copied from the source write-ups.

  • Recent years are under-observed. An accident is on file only if a later RMP submission lists it within its five-year history. Facilities whose last submission was before an accident never show it, yet are counted as observed to 2024 if still marked active, or to their last submission year otherwise. This biases recent-year rates downward, and part or all of the decline, especially in 2022-2024, may come from it. The sensitivity set limits the problem for facilities with a 2024 or later submission, but it keeps only survivors still filing, which is itself selective, and it still shows a decline. Neither set removes the bias. docs/trend.md
  • RMP-covered facilities only. The records are for facilities that file an RMP and hold a regulated substance above its threshold. Accidents at other plants, or involving unlisted substances, are absent. docs/severity.md
  • No initiating-event field. No dollar-weighted damage analysis (damage is used only as zero or above zero), no cut by initiating event beyond the contributing-cause flags above (the data has no separate initiating-event field), no accident narratives, and no check against the current EPA RMP*Info database. docs/severity.md
  • Only reportable accidents are on file. Only accidents that meet EPA's five-year-history criteria (40 CFR 68.42) are on file: a listed substance released with at least one of deaths, injuries, significant property damage, or known offsite consequences such as evacuation, sheltering, or environmental damage. Because many records qualify through an injury, the injury share is high by construction. It must not be read as the chance that an RMP-covered release injures someone, and the data holds no minor releases. docs/severity.md
