Why injury rates at small workplaces jump around

A Spanish translation of this brief is available. English

The short answerAt a small workplace, one injury more or less changes the injury rate a lot. A rate from one year is a weak way to say whether a small workplace is safe or unsafe.

What the numbers show

  • Many small workplaces report no injuries at all. In OSHA's public injury files, 75.11% of the workplace-years in the smallest size group (001-019 employees) reported zero cases. In the largest group (1000+ employees) it was 8.13%. A zero can mean a safe year. It can also mean a few lucky months.
  • One injury moves the rate a lot. Take chemical plants with Under 50 employees. The middle plant works 56,687 hours in a year. For that plant, one more injury raises the rate by 3.53 points. For a plant with 1,000+ employees, one more injury raises it by only 0.06 points.
  • The order changes from year to year. Researchers measure this with a score. A score of one means plants keep the same order every year. A score of zero means last year's order tells you nothing. For chemical plants with Under 50 employees the score is 0.314. For plants with 1,000+ employees it is 0.742.

What this means for you

  • Workers: a low or high rate at a small workplace is not proof about your own risk. Ask about the hazards you work near: machines, chemicals, lifting, heat. Ask what the employer fixed after past injuries or near misses.
  • Small employers: do not take comfort from a year with no injuries, and do not panic over a bad year. Look at several years together. Count near misses and hazards reported by workers. These show problems before someone is hurt.
  • Anyone comparing workplaces: do not rank small workplaces by one year's rate.

Limits

The zero-case shares come from OSHA establishment filings in all industries. The one-injury swing and year-to-year scores come from chemical manufacturing plants only. The data are reported by employers and have errors. These results describe patterns in groups of workplaces, not any one workplace.
